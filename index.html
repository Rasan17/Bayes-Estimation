ℹ️ What is ROPE? (Region of Practical Equivalence)

ROPE defines an interval around zero difference that represents clinical indifference or practical equivalence. In classical testing, any microscopic difference can achieve p < 0.05 simply with large sample sizes. ROPE guarantees that we only declare superiority if the credible difference exceeds this threshold.

Clinical Example: If reducing 30-day complication rates by less than ±3% (-0.03 to +0.03) does not change patient management, setting ROPE to [-0.03, +0.03] tests whether the treatment is clinically superior, practically equivalent, or inconclusive.

Methodological Interpretation Guide: What Results Mean & What They Do NOT Mean
🟢 1. Estimation Statistics Method (Bootstrap CIs & Effect Sizes)
✓ What Estimation Statistics Means
  • Risk Difference (ARR): The direct empirical difference in observed success rates is --.
  • 95% Bootstrap Confidence Interval: Over 5,000 resamples with replacement, 95% of generated intervals contain the true effect size. Its width reflects sampling precision without assuming Gaussian normality.
  • Standardized Effect Size (Cohen's h): Standardized arcsine difference of -- allows unbiased comparison across trials regardless of baseline event rates.
  • Number Needed to Treat (NNT): On average, treating -- patients with Intervention B rather than Control A produces one additional successful outcome.
✗ What Estimation Statistics Does NOT Mean
  • NOT a Bayesian Parameter Probability: A 95% Bootstrap CI does NOT mean there is a 95% probability the true parameter is inside it. That is the Bayesian HDI! Frequentist CIs describe long-run procedure coverage.
  • Crossing Zero is NOT Proof of Equality: If the 95% CI includes zero, it does NOT prove the treatments are equal. It simply means the study had insufficient sample size to rule out zero.
  • Cohen's h Does NOT Dictate Clinical Value: A "small" effect size (h = 0.2) in a fatal outcome is lifesaving, whereas a "large" effect size in a minor side effect is clinically unimportant.
  • Odds Ratio is NOT Relative Risk: If event rates are high (>10%), the Odds Ratio (--) noticeably overstates the Relative Risk (--).
🔵 2. Bayesian Analysis Method (Posterior Credibility & Decisions)
✓ What Bayesian Analysis Means
  • Probability of Superiority: There is a direct -- posterior probability that Treatment B is superior to Control A, conditional on prior clinical belief and data.
  • 95% Highest Density Interval (HDI): The narrowest credible range: there is an actual 95% probability that the true difference lies between --.
  • ROPE Clinical Decision: Explicitly evaluates whether the credible difference is practically superior, practically equivalent, or inconclusive relative to the minimal important margin.
  • Bayes Factor (BF₁₀): The data are -- times more probable under the hypothesis of a difference than under strict equality.
✗ What Bayesian Analysis Does NOT Mean
  • NOT a Frequentist P-Value: P(Superiority) = 99% does NOT equal (1 - p). It is a direct posterior belief update.
  • Superiority Does NOT Mean 100% Patient Benefit: Even when P(B > A) is 99%, individual patient responses vary due to biological dispersion.
  • ROPE Overlap is NOT Proof of Equivalence: Equivalence is confirmed ONLY if the full 95% HDI lies strictly inside the ROPE. Overlap indicates an inconclusive/underpowered study.
  • BF is NOT the Posterior Probability of H₁: The Bayes Factor is an evidence multiplier (likelihood ratio); it only equals posterior odds if prior odds were 50:50.

ℹ️ What is ROPE? (Region of Practical Equivalence)

ROPE defines the boundary below which continuous differences are considered clinically trivial (e.g., within measurement noise or below the Minimal Clinically Important Difference [MCID]).

Clinical Example: On a 0–10 visual analog pain scale, a score change of ±0.5 to ±1.0 point is often imperceptible to patients. Setting ROPE to [-1.0, +1.0] prevents declaring an undetectable pain reduction as meaningful superiority.

Methodological Interpretation Guide: What Results Mean & What They Do NOT Mean
🟢 1. Estimation Statistics Method (Bootstrap CIs & Effect Sizes)
✓ What Estimation Statistics Means
  • Unstandardized Mean Difference: Point estimate in native clinical units: --.
  • 95% Bootstrap Confidence Interval: 5,000 resamples with replacement produce an empirical, distribution-free interval for the difference and standardized effect size.
  • Standardized Effect Size (Cohen's d & Hedges' g): Standardized effect of -- measures separation in pooled standard deviation units.
  • Common Language Effect Size (CLES): There is a -- probability that a randomly chosen patient from Group B will score higher than a randomly chosen patient from Group A.
✗ What Estimation Statistics Does NOT Mean
  • NOT a Parameter Probability: A frequentist Bootstrap CI describes the long-run coverage of the resampling procedure, NOT the probability that the true parameter is within this specific interval.
  • Small Effect is NOT Clinically Useless: A "small" Cohen's d (0.2) in a critical endpoint (e.g. stroke deficit or mortality) can be practice-changing.
  • Wide CI Spanning Zero is NOT Equivalence: Crossing zero merely indicates insufficient power to demonstrate an effect (absence of evidence != evidence of absence).
  • Does NOT Guarantee Every Individual Improves: Group-level mean separation does not eliminate individual response variation.
🔵 2. Bayesian Analysis Method (Posterior Credibility & Decisions)
✓ What Bayesian Analysis Means
  • Posterior Probability Distribution: Complete Bayesian probability updating for group means, unequal variances, and difference parameters.
  • 95% Highest Density Interval (HDI): There is an actual 95% probability that the true population difference lies within this exact range.
  • ROPE Clinical Verdict: Directly determines if the posterior probability distribution lies completely outside (superiority) or completely inside (practical equivalence) the clinical threshold.
  • Bayes Factor (BF₁₀): The data are -- times more likely under H₁ than under H₀.
✗ What Bayesian Analysis Does NOT Mean
  • NOT a P-Value: Bayesian posterior probability does not reference hypothetical unobserved data under a null hypothesis.
  • ROPE Overlap is NOT Proof of Equivalence: Overlapping the ROPE margin means the clinical trial is inconclusive, NOT that the treatments are identical.
  • BF₁₀ is NOT the Probability of H₁: The Bayes Factor is a likelihood multiplier, not the posterior probability of the hypothesis itself.
  • Prior Sensitivity: In small trials, posterior conclusions can be influenced by strong priors; testing robust/skeptical priors is essential.

Clinical Diagnostic Interpretation Guide
✓ What These Results Mean
  • Post-Test Probability: The probability a patient truly has the pathology after taking the diagnostic test, combining pre-test clinical suspicion with test accuracy.
  • Likelihood Ratio Impact: An LR+ > 10 substantially increases disease probability; an LR- < 0.1 substantially decreases disease probability.
✗ What These Results Do NOT Mean
  • Sensitivity is NOT Positive Predictive Value: A test with 95% sensitivity can still have a poor positive predictive value if the pre-test disease prevalence in that clinic is low.
  • Negative Test is NOT an Absolute Rule-Out: Unless LR- is extremely small and pre-test suspicion was low, a negative test does not guarantee disease absence.
